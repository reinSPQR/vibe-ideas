## Per-idea notes

1. **Everdell Tree-Spine Card Browser & Berry Silo** — Demand for Everdell/cozy games is real (verified via GameSpot/BGG cozy-game coverage), but the idea's own differentiation claim ("existing Everdell prints are box-storage trays, not in-play browsing aids") is directly false: Printables' "Everdell Card Holders" and "Special Event Card Stand" are explicitly in-play browsing aids, and MakerWorld's free "Everdell - resource holders" set already includes a literal tree-trunk-shaped amber/resource holder — the near-exact concept here. Margin and producibility (2 parts, clear tolerances) are solid; differentiation is not.

2. **Cozy Games Nesting Hex Canister Set** — Highest score this turn. Cozy-game trend is genuinely confirmed (GameSpot 2026 list, BGG trend blog). Differentiation is real but narrower than claimed: "HexNest" magnetic modular hex trays and stacking hex-bowl sets already exist as game-agnostic hex token storage, so the gap is specifically the closed-lid nest-to-one-volume travel format, not the hex-token-storage category itself. Margin and producibility are both clean, well-toleranced 4-part designs.

3. **Print-in-Place Zero-Hardware Dice Vault** — Dice-tower demand is well established. But the differentiation claim ("every existing design requires the user to glue in loose magnets") is contradicted by search: multiple existing print-in-place folding dice towers already ship with zero magnets and zero hardware (GrandFather's no-magnet variant, Amdrin's PiP tower, "Collapsible Dice Tower" explicitly "no magnets, no clips"). This is a commodity sub-category already, not a gap. Producibility docked slightly for the self-flagged living-hinge reliability risk.

4. **Woodland Critter Sculpted Meeple Upgrade Set** — Demand real (established Etsy meeple-upgrade category, cozy trend). Differentiation capped at 6/15: single-species animal meeples (fox, gorilla, sheep, elephant, penguin, snake) are already a standing Etsy pattern, and unofficial Everdell-specific animal-upgrade sets already exist, so a 6-species matched set is an incremental variation on an established format rather than a new angle. Best margin and clean producibility in the set.

5. **Box-Edge Clip-On Score Dial** — Demand for score trackers is a confirmed established category, though most listed products are flat/standing designs rather than specifically cross-title. No direct "clips onto the box lid edge" competitor was found in search (some general mention of clip-style trackers, no concrete matching product), so the differentiation angle is plausible and not directly contradicted — scored on the higher end of "inconclusive" rather than assumed clear. Producibility docked slightly for the PETG spring-jaw arm being a flex-fatigue risk noted but not mitigated.

6. **Shared-Table Two-Player Hands-Free Card Fan Tray** — Demand for hands-free/accessibility card holders is well confirmed (caregiver/assisted-living market). Differentiation claim ("no existing holder combines two racks with a privacy divider") is directly contradicted: MakerWorld's "Playing card holder (divideable)" is already described as a two-part design "curved... to ensure other players cannot see your cards" — the same core mechanism. Cleanest single-part producibility spec in the set.

7. **Tool-Free Interlocking Modular Dungeon Tile Set** — Modular dungeon terrain is one of the best-established demand categories checked this turn. But the differentiation claim ("every existing system relies on magnets/pins the user installs") is false: OpenLOCK's clip system and "Masterful Dungeon Tiles – Clip Connectors" are established, actively-used tool-free/magnet-free connector systems. This is a real, populated sub-category, not a gap.

8. **Tuck Box Butler** — Lowest score this turn. The rationale's core demand claim — "2026 cozy/filler-game coverage repeatedly highlighting" a growth segment for small-box filler/trick-taking games — could not be found in search; nothing in GameSpot/industry coverage tied filler games to a 2026 growth trend the way claimed. Per rubric, an unverifiable trend claim caps demand, and this one is weak enough to score below the cap. Differentiation is also softer than claimed: a dedicated Sushi Go Party insert and Etsy tuck-box organizers "with draw & discard trays all in one" already exist, so the niche isn't as unaddressed as stated. Margin and producibility (simple 1-part caddy) are fine.

9. **Adjustable-Arm Rotating Turn-Order Carousel** — Turn-order marker demand is a confirmed established cross-title category. Differentiation is moderate and not directly contradicted: rotating-dial turn trackers with numbered slots exist, but the specific fold-away 2-6-player arm mechanism plus a simultaneous current/next-player dual indicator wasn't matched by any single found product — the best-supported differentiation claim in the set. Producibility is the main risk here: 6 independent print-in-place hinge-and-detent joints on one build is a meaningfully more failure-prone mechanism than this turn's simpler single- or two-part designs, and the notes don't flag or mitigate that multi-joint risk.

10. **Expandable Modular Sleeved-Card Library System** — Sleeved-card storage demand is real and well established. Differentiation claim ("existing bookshelf/vault boxes are fixed-capacity, not designed as an expandable system") is directly contradicted: MakerWorld's "Stack Box Infinite" is marketed with almost the identical pitch ("combine with any number of others — as tall, as wide, and as colorful as you want"), and other modular TCG storage systems with grid-clip connectors already exist. Margin is the weakest in the set even taken at face value — $17 for a 150g/7hr single module is a thin per-unit margin resting entirely on an unproven repeat-purchase assumption the rationale doesn't otherwise support.

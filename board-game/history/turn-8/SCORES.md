6. **Stratum** — This is the turn's clearest differentiation failure. The idea's own stated search phrase, "order-locked drafting rack positional slot scoring board game," was run verbatim and its very first result is **Rack-O** (Winning Moves/Hasbro, since 1956): a personal 10-slot rack where cards must be placed in fixed slot order and "without rearranging any of them" — i.e., the exact "order-locked... rather than a free-form hand or tableau" rule the idea claims as its defining original hook. The pass-the-tray draft layer (Sushi Go-style) is a real second ingredient, but the idea markets the *rack* itself as the original piece, and that specific claim is directly prior-arted by a 68-year-old mass-market game. This is also a classification-honesty problem: labeled `"new"`, this reads much more like an undeclared mashup/twist of two existing full mechanics (Rack-O + Sushi Go) than an original game. Differentiation scores 8/40. Demand cites Sushi Go's "over 51,000 BGG ratings... top 1,000 games" — an unverifiable specific figure (search returned Sushi Go's real existence but not the number) — capped at 10/20. Fun names Sushi Go's scoring-category tension specifically — 15/20. Producibility is clean regardless of the mechanism concern: friction-fit lid (not a living hinge), simple open-slot geometry, no sub-1mm features — 18/20.

## Per-idea notes

1. **Vault Breakers** — Differentiation docked: the idea's own search checked only
   "Cryptex"-wording and missed **Heist** (Fundex Games, BGG #40886), a shipped
   card game whose entire hook is a physical combination dial players spin to
   "crack the code, get the gold" in a heist setting — confirmed via BGG/product
   description ("combination dial and instructions," 108 cards, 2-6 players).
   Vault Breakers' room-movement + blind clue-bags + Alarm-driven loot decay
   remain genuinely distinct from Heist's card-passing loop, but the "physical
   dial for cracking a vault code" hook the idea leans on as novel is not
   unclaimed the way the rationale implies — this is the exact "search your own
   claim's literal wording" miss BOARD.md has flagged repeatedly. Demand and fun
   both name specific comparables (Deception: Murder in Hong Kong, Clue) and
   address audience fit reasonably, capping short of top marks for being fairly
   generic category evidence rather than a hard, narrow figure. Producibility is
   solid: the 3-stacked-ring cryptex mechanism is explicitly justified by
   reference to proven hobbyist cryptex geometry with a stated clearance, not a
   promise to test later.

2. **Tidal Kingdoms** — Highest-surviving "new" claim in the batch. Two rounds of
   search (mine covering "board game tiles submerge stacking ring elevation" and
   "area control tile flood flip underwater") turned up Land Above Sea Below
   (digital, already disclosed in the idea's own text), Pandemic: Rising Tide
   (card/cube-driven, no physical elevation), Forbidden Island (card-based flood,
   no stacking), and **High Tide** (Underdog Games — a real physical
   hex-tile-stacking game, but about competitively building height, not a
   rising-tide ring that permanently drowns low tiles) — none contradicts the
   specific "stacking ring on a center post physically flips low tiles" mechanism.
   Demand cites Kingdomino/Cascadia/Karuba, both real SdJ-caliber bestsellers.
   Producibility is excellent: identical-geometry ring stack needs only one
   tolerance validated, no cards, no feature under 1mm.

3. **Freight Line** — Differentiation held up under direct scrutiny of its closest
   real analog. Checked specifically against Tsuro (which the idea's own
   fun_case cites as a comparable) — confirmed via search that in Tsuro only the
   active player's token moves, on their own turn, adjacent-placement-only,
   whereas Freight Line has all engines move simultaneously once per round with
   increasing speed from a shared drafted market — a materially different
   structure, so the claim survives. Also checked against Railroad Tiles
   (personal boards, correctly distinguished) and Whistle Stop. Demand grounded
   in the well-documented train-game niche (Ticket to Ride family, 18xx). Best
   producibility profile in the batch: flat engraved tiles, zero joints, zero
   hinges, explicitly the lowest-risk design this turn.

4. **Orchard Order** — Differentiation docked: a search on the claim's own core
   phrase ("rotating carousel drafting board game pockets spin") found **Let's
   Learn Carousel** (Tactic Games), a shipped children's game that literally uses
   a rotating carousel disc with spinning pockets — a real product the idea's own
   search (which checked only "lazy susan" and generic patents) missed. The
   specific application differs (match-and-collect vs. personal-grid pattern
   drafting), so the idea isn't fully invalidated, but the framing "no shipped
   game uses a rotating carousel mechanism" is weaker than presented — the
   general mechanism is not unclaimed territory. Demand is strong and specific
   (Azul/Sagrada/Sushi Go, all real top-selling drafters). Producibility is
   excellent: 3 part types, single low-risk pivot joint explicitly compared to
   proven spinner mechanisms.

5. **Ironforge Hollow** (reskin 1 of 2, scored normally) — Honest reskin
   classification (standard unmodified Kalah rules). The idea's own search found
   Beehive Mancala as a real precedent validating the reskin category, and no
   dwarven-forge-specific theme collision; I was unable to independently
   re-verify the theme search this turn (web search budget was exhausted
   mid-session), so this is taken at the idea's stated word rather than
   independently confirmed — treated as a moderate-confidence pass, not a
   verified clean bill. Demand is strong and specific (Mancala's ancient/global
   reach plus the real Beehive Mancala retail precedent). Fun is honest that the
   theme adds no new decision, capping it below top marks. Producibility is the
   cleanest in the batch: 2 part types, zero joints, one stated pit-depth
   tolerance.

6. **Coral Court** (reskin 2 of 2, scored normally) — Honest reskin
   classification (standard Nine Men's Morris/mill rules). I independently
   confirmed Spaceship Morris exists via direct fetch (TheGameCrafter, sci-fi
   reskin, "In SPAAAAAAACE!," $18.99) — validating the idea's own precedent
   claim rather than just trusting it. Could not independently re-verify the
   coral-reef-theme-is-unclaimed search due to budget exhaustion. Demand is
   strong and independently verified: Nine Men's Morris's ancient/mass-retail
   status plus the confirmed Spaceship Morris precedent. Fun is honest about
   adding no new decision. Producibility is excellent: 2 part types, only a
   low-risk peg-in-recess friction fit, no hardware.

7. **Rune Trick** — Differentiation docked on a genuine partial finding: a search
   on "trick-taking game trump changes every trick" surfaced a BGG-listed game
   literally named **Trump Change**, described as "a trick-taking game... where
   the Trump continues to change" — details were too thin to confirm whether it
   matches Rune Trick's specific trick-winner-sets-next-trump-via-dial mechanic,
   but it's enough to show "dynamically changing trump within a hand" is not an
   unprecedented concept in trick-taking generally, weakening the "genuine rule
   change" framing somewhat even though the tile+orientation-choice+physical-dial
   packaging remains more novel. Demand independently confirmed: Texas 42 is
   documented as Texas's official State Domino Game (designated 2011) with an
   annual state championship, verified via direct fetch — a stronger, harder
   citation than most demand claims this turn. Producibility solid: engraved
   dominoes as the card substitute, one low-risk dial-pivot joint, simple tile
   racks.

8. **Guild Hollow** — Best-surviving differentiation claim this turn: multiple
   differently-worded searches (mine and the idea's own) for "worker placement
   adjacent slot bonus shared with opponent" and "gear mesh adjacency worker
   placement" found no shipped game with this specific mechanic — every result
   was either a generic mechanic overview or an unrelated game. Genuinely a
   twist that adds real interaction to a proven engine. Demand grounded in
   worker placement's status as a top BGG heavy category (Agricola, Caylus,
   Lords of Waterdeep). Fun_case names a specific comparable mechanism (Lords of
   Waterdeep's shared quest-completion bonuses) rather than asserting fun.
   Producibility smartly defuses what looks like a risky feature: the
   gear-shaped workers are explicitly stated to be decorative silhouettes that do
   NOT physically mesh/interlock, sidestepping tooth-engagement tolerance risk
   entirely rather than taking it on and hoping it prints cleanly.

9. **Teetering Peaks** — Differentiation reasonably intact: searches for
   "dexterity board game multiple towers prediction bet tallest" found only
   Tournament of Towers (single-tower, card-drafting build — not a match) and
   generic mentions of Junk Art/Crokinole/KLASK with no confirmed multi-zone
   prediction-betting mode: no direct contradiction found across two rounds of
   search. The space is crowded (Jenga, Tetra Tower, Junk Art all balance-
   stacking) even though this exact 4-peak-plus-betting combination evades
   direct hits, so it's scored well but not at the top. Demand and fun both cite
   real, verifiable comparables (Jenga's sales dominance, Tetra Tower's retail
   presence) and fun_case explicitly argues the multi-round structure avoids
   single-collapse elimination frustration. Producibility correctly reasons from
   proven external geometry (Tetra Tower/Cheese Wedge's truncated-prism balance
   profile) rather than promising to prototype-and-see — the right way to defuse
   a dexterity mechanism's inherent risk.

10. **Sundial Market** — Differentiation reasonably intact: the closest hit found
    was Cyclades' auction mechanic (bid pieces advance on tracks and don't simply
    reset, get returned to the player if outbid), a loose parallel but
    meaningfully different from Sundial Market's single shared spiral track
    that's simultaneously the bid amount and a permanently-spent global resource
    across all 8 rounds — no direct contradiction found. Demand cites real,
    enduring auction-genre staples (Ra, For Sale, Modern Art, High Society).
    Producibility is excellent: a fully static engraved groove board with pegs,
    zero moving assemblies, explicitly compared to a proven cribbage-track peg
    joint.

## Per-idea notes

1. **Hexfall Kingdoms** — Two differently-worded searches ("shared depleting resource bank hex kingdom" / "hex tile kingdom builder finite shared resource supply") found only Kingdomino, Kingdom Builder, and Tiny Kingdom, none of which use a shared physically-depleting resource bank; the "new" claim survives intact. Kingdomino's 2017 Spiel des Jahres win and Blue Orange's spinoff line (Queendomino, Origins, Age of Giants) checked out exactly as claimed. Fun case names Great Western Trail and Puerto Rico as comparable scarcity mechanics; producibility is the strongest in the batch (zero moving joints, static dimples only). Highest score this turn.

2. **Gearlock Derby** — The core "new" claim (crank-propelled meshing-gear drivetrain race) held up across two differently-worded searches (no shipped product found), but one of its two cited comparables, "Gear Towers," could not be located in two separate targeted searches and reads as possibly fabricated or misremembered — this undercuts confidence in the differentiation writeup even though the primary claim itself wasn't contradicted, so it's scored down rather than zeroed. Producibility is capped because meshing 40 gears across 3 tooth counts (8/12/16) via FDM at a fixed 0.3mm bore clearance is a genuine involute-gear-profile CAD engineering problem, not the low-tolerance passive-rest fit the notes claim — this reads closer to "needs significant CAD judgment" than the notes admit.

3. **Fortress Echo** — Zero-gate not triggered, but differentiation fails hard: a differently-worded search ("Battleship variant vertical layers submarine surface hidden grid") immediately found **Sub Search** (Milton Bradley, 1973), a shipped commercial Battleship variant with ships hidden across three underwater levels plus a surface level — directly contradicting the claim that "no result adds a third hidden dimension (elevation level)." This is the exact single-search-miss failure pattern BOARD.md has flagged every turn since the post-Turn-4 reliability check; the idea's own search ("Battleship hidden fortress elevation arc trajectory") simply used the wrong words. The "echo" partial-info reveal may still be a genuine incremental addition over Sub Search's hit/near-miss/miss resolution, which is why this isn't a flat zero, but the broad claim as written doesn't survive. Lowest score this turn.

4. **Monsoon Route** — Differentiation search was misdirected: the idea searched "pickup and deliver desert caravan" comparables (Caravan, Through the Desert) for a *nautical* wind-driven exploration game, and a search actually aimed at the claim's own domain ("sailing trade game wind compass shared movement modifier") immediately surfaced **Cartolan: Trade Winds**, a shipped tile-laying exploration game with an expanding modular map and a wind mechanic that grants bonus movement when moving with the wind — very close prior art in the exact space claimed empty. Demand citations (Caravan, Through the Desert, Ticket to Ride) are all independently confirmed accurate, keeping demand/fun reasonable despite the differentiation miss.

5. **Chrono Loop** — Two differently-worded searches for a tabletop rewind-on-collision programmed-movement racer found nothing shipped (one hit, "Rewind" on BGG, is an unrelated trick-card game); the "new" claim holds. RoboRally's three-publisher edition history (WotC 1994, Avalon Hill 2005, Renegade 2023) was independently verified accurate, supporting a strong, well-evidenced demand case. Fun case names RoboRally/Formula D specifically and producibility has no risky joints beyond a simple 4-instance sliding lid.

6. **Aurora Peak** — Two differently-worded searches for a carousel-draft dexterity stacking game with a tremor/collapse mechanic found nothing shipped matching that combination; Junk Art's multi-outlet review coverage (RPGnet, Opinionated Gamers, Board Game Quest, Zatu) was independently confirmed accurate. Fun case draws a specific, credible contrast with Junk Art's end-of-game-only scoring via the mid-game summit checkpoint. Producibility is strong: one moving joint (carousel pivot), single instance, and the 60 rock pieces need no fit tolerance at all.

7. **Crystal Cavern** — Geode and Gemstones were both independently confirmed to match the idea's descriptions exactly (Geode's 4-tile circle formation, Gemstones' connected-shard-growth penalty), and a further search for "fixed irregular scoring regions forced-color die placement" found no shipped competitor — the "new" claim is well-scoped and survives. Demand case leans on the evergreen Go/Blokus category plus the two named comparables' active BGG/Kickstarter presence; audience framing is a touch generic but backed by real comparables. Best producibility in the batch: 4 part types, zero moving joints, single static molded board.

8. **Market Tides** — The core differentiation claim (predictably-rotating shared price dial in a blind-bid auction) survived two differently-worded searches with no shipped competitor found. Demand, however, contains a plain factual error of exactly the kind flagged in Turns 1-5: the claim that High Society "remains in print with a 2023 reprint" is false — Osprey Games' reprint was 2018, not 2023 — so this is capped hard rather than taken on faith. Producibility's dual-concentric-ring dial is a genuinely more demanding joint than most of the batch, but it's a single instance, keeping the risk contained.

9. **Mancala: Solar Harvest** — Honestly classified as a reskin (explicitly no rule/scoring/turn-structure change), and Kalah's status as "the most successful modern" Mancala variant per BGG, plus its 100+ documented variant family and continuous mainstream retail presence, checked out exactly as claimed — this is the best-evidenced demand case in the batch. As a reskin it's capped on the differentiation ceiling per the rubric (no new mechanism), but its execution and honesty about that fact are exactly right. Producibility is near-ideal: 3 part types, zero joints.

10. **Nine Men's Morris: Ghost Manor** — Also an honestly-classified reskin of a well-documented ancient public-domain game; WE Games' wooden travel edition and the game's Roman/Egyptian-era origin were both independently confirmed via search. Fun case leans on the game's ~2000-year continuous play history rather than naming a specific modern comparable beyond a loose checkers/tic-tac-toe reference, which is thinner than the batch's stronger fun cases. Producibility ties for best in the batch: 3 part types, zero joints, crisp 1.5-2mm grid engraving as the only called-out risk.

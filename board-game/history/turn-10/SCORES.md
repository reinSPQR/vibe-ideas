## Per-idea notes

1. **Beacon Watch** — Differentiation reduced to 10/40: a targeted search
   for "lighthouse smuggler hidden movement" immediately surfaced **Insel
   der Schmuggler** (Haba), a shipped 2-4 player game whose entire premise
   is a physical rotating lighthouse beam (die-controlled) that catches
   smuggler boats carrying hidden-destination cargo — precisely the
   headline mechanism ("physical rotating beam disc catches a smuggler")
   this idea's differentiation section leans on to distinguish itself from
   Scotland Yard/Specter Ops. The hidden private-tracking-board layer is a
   real addition on top of that, but the core hook is not novel. Demand
   (Scotland Yard's real, long print run) and fun_case (named comparables)
   are solid; producibility is clean (one reasoned detent joint, no
   "needs testing" admissions).

2. **Terraform Ridge** — Highest score this turn (81). Two searches for
   "3D elevation relief tile placement adjacency" and "raised topographic
   Kingdomino elevation" found no contradiction — the elevation-adjacency
   rule swap on a Kingdomino base is a genuine, physically-enabled rule
   twist. Kingdomino's 2017 Spiel des Jahres win was independently
   re-verified and is correct. Fun_case names Kingdomino explicitly;
   producibility flags its one tight tolerance (3mm elevation steps) with
   a concrete fallback (4mm base) rather than an open question.

3. **Silo Stack** — Differentiation reduced to 18/40: a search for
   "stacking tower push your luck dice bank tokens collapse" surfaced
   **PUSH** (KOSMOS), a real published game combining physical stacking
   with a bank-or-continue push-your-luck bust curve — the idea's own
   rationale flagged Bausack as worth checking but missed this closer
   analog. Producibility capped at 10/20 per the standing Turn 8 rule: the
   wobble-tray pivot boss, the mechanism the entire game's core turn
   depends on, is explicitly described as needing "a physical
   test-and-tune pass before locking dimensions" — an admitted-unresolved
   core mechanism, not a mitigated one, regardless of the concrete
   clearance numbers attached.

4. **Dome of Kings** — Differentiation reduced to 16/40: a search for
   "spherical Go curved wraparound board game" found that "Spherical Go"
   is an established, independently-implemented concept (multiple named
   GitHub/web projects reproducing Go's enclosure rules on a sphere) — the
   idea's own rationale specifically flagged this as the risk to check,
   and the check confirms the conceptual novelty is weaker than claimed,
   even though no *physical, shipped* dome board game was found (the
   FDM-manufacturing angle is real). This reads closer to an undisclosed
   twist on Go than a genuinely "new" abstract. Demand (Hex, GIPF) and
   producibility (low part count, no risky joints, reasoned normal-offset
   hole placement) remain strong.

5. **Current Drop** — Two searches ("vertical spiral tower push-your-luck
   diving current wheel" and "Deep Sea Adventure vertical variant") found
   no contradicting prior art; the twist on Deep Sea Adventure's shared-
   oxygen push-your-luck core survives verification. Deep Sea Adventure's
   real Oink Games pedigree is accurately cited. Producibility reasons the
   center bearing peg as a validated lazy-susan joint type rather than
   promising to test it, which is the correct pattern per Turn 8's rule.

6. **Rune Forge** — Differentiation is the strongest of the batch (32/40):
   two targeted searches for "notched tile interlock legal combination"
   and "puzzle-piece edges enforce valid scoring set" found nothing
   resembling this notch-encodes-rule-legality mechanism. Producibility
   capped at 10/20, however: the notes explicitly call for "physically
   testing all 5x5 notch-profile pairings on a physical test print before
   locking the full 60-tile geometry" for the game's entire core mechanic
   (sigil validity checks happen most turns) — an admitted-unvalidated
   core mechanism per the Turn 8 hard-cap rule, despite the precise 0.3mm
   interference-fit number attached.

7. **Clockwork Court** — Differentiation holds (30/40): searches for
   "gear mesh hidden role social deduction" and "secret handshake tactile
   token trust game" found only a verbally-themed "Secret Handshake" party
   game, not a gear-token mechanism match. Demand is the strongest in the

   "physically test all gear pairs before locking geometry, since this is
   the core information-delivery mechanism of the game" — an admitted-open
   validation question on the mechanic the entire hidden-role structure
   depends on.

8. **Volcano Climbers** — Disclosed reskin (1st of 2, scored on its own
   merits). Differentiation reduced to 12/40: a search for "Can't Stop
   licensed editions" found Playte Games already publishes mountain-
   climbing-themed editions of Can't Stop (including a "Mount Fuji"
   edition), meaning the "sculpted rising-column mountain-climb" styling
   angle is not as distinct as claimed, even though a volcano/lava theme
   specifically wasn't found. Can't Stop's Sid Sackson/1978 provenance is
   accurate. Producibility is the cleanest in the set — zero joints beyond
   pegs resting loosely in notches.

9. **Beehive Harvest** — Disclosed reskin (2nd of 2, scored on its own
   merits, not the forced-zero cap). Differentiation is the lowest score
   this turn (5/40): a search for "beehive honeycomb mancala bee themed"
   immediately found **Beehive Mancala: A Nature Board Game** (Laurence
   King Publishing, illustrated by Tatiana Boyko), sold through Kew
   Gardens' shop, Walmart, eBay, and Zatu Games — "bees collecting pollen
   from flowers back to the hive to make honey," an almost word-for-word
   match to this idea's theme and mechanic. The idea's own rationale
   flagged this exact risk ("high thematic saturation... plausible-but-
   unverified styling niche") and recommended a live check before
   commitment; the check finds the niche is already filled. Producibility
   remains excellent (zero joints, tokens rest loosely in open pits).

Searched "shared heat gauge peg track worker placement forging board game" and "blacksmith push your luck shared overheat track board game"; found only generic worker-placement lists and Heat: Pedal to the Metal (a racing deckbuilder with a private, not shared, overheat mechanic) — no contradiction of the claimed shared/collective heat-gauge mechanic.
3. **Compass Trick** — Not built this turn — Differentiation only. Searched "trump suit determined by highest card played trick-taking board game" and "automatic trump change every trick highest tile trick-taking game"; surfaced two real adjacent precedents the idea's own text didn't mention — Sheng ji (trump changes, but per round via bid/rank progression, not per-trick via highest card) and The Fox in the Forest (a "revolving trump suit that can be changed by the players," i.e. player-initiated, not automatic/deterministic). Neither is an exact match to Compass Trick's fully deterministic, mandatory post-trick trump swing, but the "changing trump" niche is busier than the idea's own differentiation text (which cited only Marjapussi) acknowledged — scored down moderately for this gap rather than fully credited.
4. **Terrace Stack** — Not built this turn — Differentiation only. **Idea 6 fidelity detail (why 2/20, not higher):** `cad_prompt` specified 4 distinct blue 6x6 grid boards, 5 white cube dice with engraved shape icons, 60 flat polyomino tiles across 4 colors (red/yellow/green/purple), and a separate two-piece navy/white storage box — 4 clearly separate part types with real color variation. The actual build (`photo.jpg`, `assembled.png`, `qa.png`, all cross-checked and consistent) is a **single fused, monochrome teal block** shaped like the storage box, with the grid pattern, a small die-like nub, and a couple of flat tile-shaped ridges merely engraved/embossed into its lid — no loose boards, no loose dice, no loose colored tiles exist as separate objects anywhere in the model. This is the most severe component-collapse case built under this pipeline to date: not a dropped or merged pair of parts, but essentially all 4 part types compressed into one non-functional single-color prop. The 2/20 credit reflects that the grid motif and general "cargo box" silhouette are still vaguely recognizable, not that any component genuinely survived intact. Printability scored a perfect 10/10 specifically *because* it's now one simple monolithic solid — a direct illustration of the standing "easy to print is not evidence of a good build" risk: extreme part-consolidation trivially maximizes printability while destroying concept fidelity.
